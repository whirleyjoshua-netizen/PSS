# Premier Shade Solutions — Business Formation Checklist

**Prepared:** 2026-08-26
**Jurisdiction:** Nevada — Las Vegas / Clark County

> This is an organized research summary and action checklist, not legal or tax advice. Two items in Phase 3 involve real judgment calls (contractor licensing and sales tax treatment) where a one-time conversation with a Nevada CPA or business attorney is worth the money. Fees and rules change — verify each on the linked official site before you pay.

---

## Why this matters right now

Alta Window Fashions, like every manufacturer dealer program, will not open a wholesale account without proof you are a real business. Expect them to ask for:

- Legal business name and entity type
- **EIN** (federal tax ID)
- **Nevada resale certificate / sales tax permit** — this is the one that lets them sell to you tax-free, and it is non-negotiable
- Business license
- Business address and phone
- Sometimes: trade references or a credit application

Phases 1 through 3 below produce every one of those documents. Do them in order — each step needs the one before it.

## Phase 1 — Decide the structure

### 1.1 Entity type

**Recommended: Nevada LLC, member-managed, with both of you as members.**

Why an LLC rather than operating as a sole proprietorship or general partnership: you will be in customers' homes, on ladders, drilling into walls, and installing motorized products wired near windows. A damaged hardwood floor or a fall claim against a sole proprietor reaches your personal assets — your savings, your vehicles, your home. An LLC is the separation between business liability and personal liability, and at $425 it is the cheapest insurance you will ever buy.

Nevada is also one of the better states for this: **no state personal income tax, no corporate income tax, no franchise tax.**

**A note specific to the two of you:** you are engaged, not yet married. A two-member LLC between unmarried partners is treated as a **partnership** by the IRS by default, which means filing a Form 1065 partnership return each year and issuing K-1s to yourselves. That is more paperwork and a higher tax-prep bill than a single-member LLC. Two things worth knowing:

- Once married, a married couple's LLC in a **community property state — which Nevada is** — can elect to be treated as a disregarded entity and file on your joint personal return instead. Considerably simpler.
- Whether to make an **S-corp election** later, once profit is consistent, is a real money question — it can save meaningful self-employment tax above roughly $50–60k of net profit, at the cost of running payroll. Do not do this at formation. Revisit with a CPA after your first profitable year.

**Action:** decide member-managed (both of you can sign for the business) versus manager-managed. For a two-person shop, member-managed is standard.

### 1.2 Name availability

Confirm "Premier Shade Solutions LLC" is available before anything else.

- Search the Nevada business name database at [SilverFlume](https://www.nvsilverflume.gov/)
- Also search the [USPTO trademark database](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search) for conflicts — a state name registration is not trademark clearance
- You already hold `premiershadesolutions.com`, which is good evidence of use, and worth grabbing the matching social handles now even if you will not post yet

If the exact name is taken, a variation plus a DBA is a normal fallback.

### 1.3 Registered agent

Nevada requires a registered agent with a physical Nevada street address, available during business hours, whose address becomes **public record.**

- **You can be your own agent** — free, but your home address goes on the public internet, and you must be there to receive service of process.
- **Commercial agent** — roughly $50–150/year, keeps your home address private, forwards mail. For a home-based business, generally worth it.

## Phase 2 — Form the entity

### 2.1 File with the Nevada Secretary of State

Everything is done through **[SilverFlume](https://www.nvsilverflume.gov/)**, Nevada's single business portal. It bundles the state filings into one checkout.

| Filing | Fee | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Articles of Organization | **$75** | Creates the LLC |
| Initial List of Managers/Members | **$150** | Due at formation |
| State Business License | **$200** | Annual, required of nearly every NV business |
| **Total to form** | **$425** | |

Recurring: **$350/year** ($150 annual list + $200 license renewal), due by the last day of your formation anniversary month. Missing it brings penalties and eventually revocation — put it on the calendar the day you file.

Approval is typically a few business days online; expedite options exist for an extra fee.

### 2.2 Operating agreement

Nevada does not require one. **Write one anyway.** It is the single most important document for a two-person business, and skipping it is the most common expensive mistake couples make.

It should state, in writing:

- Ownership percentages (50/50, or otherwise)
- Who contributed what in startup capital, and whether that is a loan to the company or equity
- How profits and draws are split, and when
- Who decides what — spending limits, who can sign contracts, how you break a tie
- What happens if one of you wants out, becomes disabled, or dies
- **What happens to the business if the relationship ends**

That last one is uncomfortable and it is exactly why it belongs in writing now, while you both want the same thing. An operating agreement written today costs a template fee or a few hundred dollars with an attorney. One negotiated during a dispute costs many thousands.

Banks will also frequently ask to see it when you open the business account.

### 2.3 EIN

Free, from the IRS, takes about ten minutes.

- Apply directly at [irs.gov](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online) — Monday–Friday, roughly 7am–10pm ET
- **Never pay a third-party service for this.** Sites charging $50–300 for an EIN are reselling a free government form.
- Required for: the business bank account, the Alta account, hiring anyone, and your tax filings

## Phase 3 — Licenses and permits

### 3.1 State business license

Included in the SilverFlume bundle above ($200/year). Done in Phase 2.

### 3.2 Clark County business license

Required if you operate in unincorporated Clark County. Apply through Clark County Business License at [clarkcountynv.gov](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/business/business_license/). Fees vary by category.

### 3.3 City license

If your business address sits inside city limits, you need the **city** license rather than or in addition to the county one:

- [City of Las Vegas](https://www.lasvegasnevada.gov/Business/Business-Licenses)
- [City of Henderson](https://www.cityofhenderson.com/business)
- [City of North Las Vegas](https://www.cityofnorthlasvegas.com/departments/business_license/)

Your physical address determines which. Confirm with the jurisdiction — the boundaries are not intuitive, and Summerlin, for instance, is partly city of Las Vegas and partly unincorporated county.

Home-based businesses generally also need a **home occupation permit**, and HOAs sometimes have their own rules about running a business from a residence.

### 3.4 Sales tax permit / resale certificate ← *required for Alta*

Register with the **Nevada Department of Taxation** at [tax.nv.gov](https://tax.nv.gov/) (also reachable through SilverFlume). This gets you a Sales and Use Tax Permit, and with it the **resale certificate** you give Alta and your local vendors so they do not charge you sales tax on goods you resell.

**The judgment call:** whether an installed window treatment is a *retail sale of tangible goods* (you collect sales tax from the customer on the product) or an *improvement to real property* (you pay tax on materials as the end consumer and do not charge the customer sales tax) depends on the product and how it is attached. Interior blinds and shades and hard-wired or permanently mounted exterior products can land on opposite sides of that line, and the answer changes what you charge on every invoice.

**Get this right before your first invoice**, because fixing it retroactively means either eating the tax or going back to customers. This is a specific question for a Nevada CPA — one billable hour, and it pays for itself.

### 3.5 Contractor's license ← *verify before selling exterior products*

This is the item I would not guess at.

Nevada requires a state contractor's license under **NRS 624** for construction work over **$1,000** in combined labor and materials. Your jobs will routinely exceed that.

There is, however, an exemption for the sale or installation of a finished product that **does not become a permanent fixed part of the structure.** That exemption plausibly covers interior blinds, shades, and shutters mounted in a window opening. Nevada also recognizes a **window coverings limited specialty** classification (referenced as C-61/D-52), which suggests some window covering work is squarely licensable.

The line matters most for the **Outdoor category** in the website plan — rolling shutters, exterior patio shades, and security screens are attached to the building, are often permitted, and are far more likely to require a license than a roller shade inside a window.

**Action:** call the [Nevada State Contractors Board](https://www.nvcontractorsboard.com/) directly, describe exactly what you install, and get the answer from them. It is a free phone call. Unlicensed contracting in Nevada is a criminal matter, not a fee, and it also voids your ability to enforce a contract for payment. If a license is required, budget for the application, exam, financial statement, and surety bond, and know that it takes real time — which is an argument for launching the website with interior products and adding the Outdoor category once you are cleared.

## Phase 4 — Money and protection

### 4.1 Business bank account

Open one **immediately** after the EIN, and run every dollar of business income and expense through it.

Mixing personal and business money is how an LLC's liability protection gets pierced in court — the legal term is "alter ego," and it means a judge treats the company as indistinguishable from you personally, which defeats the entire point of Phase 2. It also makes your taxes dramatically easier.

Bring: Articles of Organization, EIN letter, operating agreement, business licenses, and both IDs. Get a business debit card and consider a business credit card to build business credit — Alta and other vendors may eventually extend terms based on it.

### 4.2 Insurance

Non-negotiable in this trade, and vendors and higher-end clients will ask for proof.

- **General liability** — the baseline. You are drilling into other people's walls. Typically $500–1,500/year at your size.
- **Commercial auto** — a personal auto policy generally will *not* cover an accident while driving to a job with product in the vehicle. This is a very common and very expensive gap.
- **Inventory / tools coverage** — custom product sitting in a garage awaiting install is a real loss exposure.
- **Workers' comp** — Nevada requires it once you have employees. Members of an LLC can often elect out for themselves; if you hire an installer, this becomes mandatory.
- **Surety bond** — if the Contractors Board answer in 3.5 is "yes," a bond comes with it.

Get quotes from an independent agent who writes trade contractors, not a general consumer line.

### 4.3 Bookkeeping

Set this up before revenue, not after. QuickBooks Online, Wave, or Xero — any is fine; using one consistently is what matters. Track deposits from customers separately from earned revenue, since in this business you will typically collect 50% up front on product that has not shipped yet.

Keep every receipt. Vehicle mileage is a meaningful deduction in a business where you drive to every measurement and every install.

## Phase 5 — The Alta account

Once Phases 1–3 are done, you will have the packet. Expect Alta to want:

- Completed dealer application
- EIN
- Nevada resale certificate
- Business license(s)
- Business bank information or a credit application
- Possibly trade references, a showroom or business address, and minimum order commitments

**Ask about:** dealer pricing tiers and what moves you up one, freight policy and free-freight thresholds, lead times, the warranty process and who handles a defective product claim, remake/damage policy, sample and display program costs, territory expectations, and what training or certification they offer — particularly on motorization, which is where the margin and the callbacks both live.

Keep your existing local vendors. A second source protects you when Alta's lead time slips, and local vendors can often save a job that is running late.
